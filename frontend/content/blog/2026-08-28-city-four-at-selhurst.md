Crystal Palace 1-4 Manchester City on Friday night, and City's attack answered the question Gameweek 1 asked of it.

## Cherki 14, Haaland 13, Foden 9

**Cherki took 14 points in 81 minutes.** A week after producing 8 points in 27 minutes off the bench, he started and was the best player on the pitch. That is now two fixtures of returning at a rate the price does not reflect, and the minutes went the way you would expect after the first one.

**Haaland's 13** is the correction to last week. Ninety minutes, a return, bonus — the thing his 70% ownership is paying for. One blank followed by one haul is exactly the distribution premiums have, and it is why selling after the blank is usually the mistake.

**Foden 9** rounds out a fixture where City's front line took most of what was going.

Palace: **Yeremy 5** and not much else, in a game they lost by three.

## The Friday-night captain problem, in practice

Anyone who captained Haaland on Friday now watches the rest of the weekend with the decision locked. Anyone who captained elsewhere has just seen 26 points go past them before their own captain has kicked a ball.

That is not an argument against a Friday captain — Haaland returned, and the people who backed him were right. It is an argument for knowing you are making a different kind of bet when the fixture is first rather than last: you are giving up the option to change your mind, and that option has real value in a gameweek where nine fixtures follow.

## What it doesn't settle

One haul does not undo the structural point from last weekend. City won 4-1 and the best returns were a £7.5m midfielder and a £15.5m forward, in that order. Cherki has now outscored Haaland across the two gameweeks played.

If you own both, that is a good problem. If you own Haaland and were looking at the money tied up in him, Cherki is the reason the question is still open rather than closed.
