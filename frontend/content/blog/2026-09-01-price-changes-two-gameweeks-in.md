## The pattern is the same in both columns

Every player rising has produced. Every player falling has produced nothing — and four of the six on zero points did not play at all.

Sarr is the one with a straightforward explanation - he carries an injury flag. The other three do not.

That is unusual this early. Normally the first fortnight's price moves are driven by transfer speculation and ownership momentum ahead of actual returns. This time the market has simply followed the scoreboard, which suggests managers are reacting to results rather than anticipating them.

## The Arsenal squad problem

Four of the six fallers are Arsenal players: Martinelli, Gyökeres, Hincapie, Eze. Arsenal have won both games and kept two clean sheets. The points have gone to Calafiori, Saka, White, Ødegaard and Gabriel — and everyone else in that squad is being sold by the people who backed the wrong half of it.

If you own an Arsenal attacker who is not Saka or Ødegaard, you are holding a falling asset at a club that is winning. That is the most uncomfortable position in Fantasy, and waiting it out costs money every day.

## Watkins is not a price problem

He has joined Al Hilal permanently. He is not going to play a Premier League fixture this season. If he is still in your squad, this is not a call about form or fixtures — the slot is empty and it is costing you £7.8m of budget plus a falling price.

The same urgency does not apply to Gyökeres, who is at least still at the club. But two gameweeks with no minutes at a side scoring freely is a role question, and it is now two weeks old.
