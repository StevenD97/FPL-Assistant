## Positions

### CMO — "Ship discoverability now, precisely *because* of the break"

**Recommendation.** SEO foundation and analytics, both inside the first two weeks,
ahead of the break rather than after it.

**Evidence.** VERIFIED, from `company/CONTEXT.md`: `robots.txt` and `sitemap.xml` both
404; every page shares one title and one description; no OpenGraph or Twitter card tags
anywhere; **no analytics of any kind has ever recorded a page view**. Thirteen pages,
21 blog posts and a public accuracy record already exist and are simply not
discoverable.

**Why the break is the argument rather than the objection.** Search indexing is not
instant. Shipping in the first ten days means the sitemap is submitted and the pages
are indexed *through* the dead period, so xFPL arrives at GW6 on 10 October with its
assets already discoverable — instead of starting the clock then. There is no cheaper
use of three weeks in which nobody is playing.

**Analytics must land first or alongside, not after.** GW3–5, then a three-week
trough, then GW6 is a naturally segmented experiment. Without instrumentation in place
before GW3, the GW6 return has nothing to be compared against and the break is wasted
as a control.

**Impact** High — this is the only £0 acquisition channel and it compounds.
**Cost** £0 (free tiers only). **Effort** Low — two pull requests.
**Risk** Low. One real confound: a baseline measured across three gameweeks and a dead
zone is not a clean baseline. Mitigate by recording the break as an explicit segment
rather than averaging through it.
**Metrics, pre-committed** Indexed page count; entry pages; referrers; and return-visit
rate, reported separately for GW3–5, the break, and GW6.
**Confidence** High on the diagnosis. Medium on the timing benefit — that indexing
matures usefully within three weeks is an EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED expectation, not a
measured one for this site.

### CPO — "Agreed on the destination. Not in that order."

**Recommendation.** The same fortnight, reordered: fix what makes the product
untrustworthy *before* pointing acquisition at it, not after.

**The disagreement, stated plainly.** Traffic arriving at a product with no return
mechanism leaks, and this particular window maximises the leak. A manager acquired
during GW3–5 then gets **three weeks of nothing** before GW6. That is the largest
possible opportunity to forget xFPL exists, and there is currently no email, no
notification and no reminder to prevent it. Spending the window on acquisition and
none of it on the reasons to come back is a choice worth naming.

**What I am not asking for.** I am not asking for the retention loop first. It needs
email capture, which is Steven's decision and involves personal data; and with zero
recorded users I cannot design a retention feature without inventing the user it is
for. That would be an UNTESTED ASSUMPTION dressed as product work.

**What I am asking for.** The two defects that will cost us the visitors the CMO is
about to send, both of which are hours rather than days:

1. **The Premium gate.** `/squad` blocks tracking a fourth rival behind
   "🔒 Premium lifts the cap". There is no Premium, no accounts, and nothing to buy.
   A first-time visitor's first encounter with a paywall is a paywall that leads
   nowhere, in a product whose entire pitch is honesty.
2. **`recommendation_score` shown to three decimal places.** It has never been
   backtested. Precision we have not earned, sitting beside a projection we have.

**Impact** Medium–High, as loss avoidance rather than gain — unmeasurable by design.
**Cost** £0. **Effort** Very low. **Risk** Very low; both are reversible in one revert.
**Metrics** None available, and I will not invent one. This is a judgement call about
what a new visitor meets, and I am labelling it as such.
**Confidence** Low–Medium on retention specifics. High that shipping an empty paywall
to our first real traffic is a mistake.

### Head of FPL Analytics — "One gate, and an argument against my own department"

**Recommendation.** One precondition before amplification, and then *no model work
this month*.

**1 — The accuracy-figure inconsistency is a gate, not an item.** VERIFIED: the
backtest's Spearman of 0.704 is computed over all ~600 players including those who
never played; the live accuracy page reports 0.20 and 0.32 over players who actually
appeared. Both are honest alone. The flattering one is the one quoted in the README.

The CMO's plan points search traffic at the accuracy page — the differentiator, and
the one asset whose entire value is that a sceptic can check it. Amplifying it while
two incompatible figures are in circulation is how a credibility asset becomes a
credibility liability. This is a **reporting change, not a methodology change**: state
the population on every figure, publish both, and add a spread so one gameweek cannot
read as a trend. It needs no model change and therefore no methodology approval.

**2 — Do not start the Haulers research this month.** This is an argument against my
own department's most interesting problem, and the break is why. The record reaches
**five** graded gameweeks by 3 October, not eight to ten — GW6 is the fourth week of
October, and the sample I said was needed does not exist until November. Thirty days
buys three gameweeks of evidence and then three weeks of silence. Starting the tail
model now means starting it without the data that would judge it, which is precisely
the failure this role exists to prevent.

**What the break is actually good for.** Freezing, then evaluation. The freeze streak
has three opportunities in this window — GW3 (done, `2026-09-03T19:10:40Z`, commit
`a96ee3d`), GW4 and GW5 — taking the record from two reconstructed gameweeks to five,
three of them genuine pre-commitments. That is the single most valuable thing happening
in the next 30 days and **it costs nothing, because it is already automated.** Protect
it: any deploy that touches the freeze workflow between now and 18 September is the
one deploy worth being nervous about.

**Impact** High, as risk removal. **Cost** £0. **Effort** Low for the reporting fix;
zero for the freeze streak, which requires only that we do not break it.
**Risk** Low. The residual risk is the reverse: publishing a corrected, less flattering
figure just as traffic arrives. Accept it — a number a reader can check is the product.
**Confidence** High. The two figures and their populations are VERIFIED; the claim that
the Haulers path is the answer remains **UNTESTED** and must not be described otherwise.

## Disagreements

**1 · CPO vs CMO — ordering inside the fortnight.** Not whether, but what ships first.
The CMO would take the indexing lead time; the CPO would not meet new visitors with an
empty paywall. Both plans fit in two weeks. *What would settle it:* nothing available —
there is no measurement, which is itself the CMO's point.

**2 · Head of FPL Analytics vs CMO — is the accuracy page ready to be amplified?**
Analytics says not until the published figures state their population. The CMO's role
definition already concedes this: no accuracy claim ships without the Head of FPL
Analytics' sign-off. Conceded in the room rather than escalated.

**3 · Notable non-disagreement.** Nobody argued for model work this month — including
the executive who owns the model, who argued against it on sample-size grounds. That is
a genuine finding, not a suppressed disagreement: the fixture calendar makes it
near-unanimous.

## CEO evaluation

**The call.** Take the Head of FPL Analytics' gate, take the CPO's two fixes, and then
take the CMO's sequencing on the substance. In order, over 30 days:

| When | What | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–4 | Accuracy figures state their population, published both ways, with spread. Premium gate removed. `recommendation_score` removed from the UI or labelled as unvalidated. README corrected. | Analytics + CPO, delivered by CTO |
| Days 4–14 | SEO foundation (sitemap, robots, canonicals, per-page metadata, OG/Twitter cards) and analytics with four events, both live **before GW5 on 18 September** | CMO, delivered by CTO |
| Days 14–30 (the break) | Nothing shipped that touches the freeze path. Observe. Blog cadence per Steven's decision below. | CMO |
| GW6, 10 October | Read the data across three segments — GW3–5, break, GW6 — and decide the next 30 days from evidence | CEO |

**Why this order and not the CPO's or the CMO's alone.** The CPO's two fixes and the
Analytics gate are not competitors to the SEO work — together they are about four days
and they all reduce the risk that the traffic arrives at something that undermines us.
The CMO's timing argument then wins on its own terms: indexing lead time is the one
asset the break actually gives us, and it is forfeited by waiting. Nobody's position
was dropped; the cheap ones were front-loaded.

**Positions not taken, and what would reopen them.**

- **CPO — "acquisition without retention leaks, and this break maximises it."** Not
  taken as a reason to delay acquisition, because we cannot design retention for users
  we have never observed. Recorded as the primary risk of this plan.
  *Reopening trigger:* if the GW6 reading shows a return-visit rate below a floor Steven
  and the CPO agree in advance, the retention loop moves to the front of the next 30
  days regardless of what else is in flight.
- **Head of FPL Analytics — the Haulers gap.** Deferred on the department's own
  recommendation, not overruled. *Reopening trigger:* the record reaching roughly eight
  to ten graded gameweeks, which the calendar puts in November.
- **CTO — observability, and the free-tier capacity question.** Both real and both
  deferred: neither changes what happens in a window with no traffic to observe.
  *Reopening trigger:* the first measured traffic, or any incident.

**Explicitly not recommended for these 30 days.** Model or methodology changes ·
starting the tail-model research · any paid infrastructure · accounts or email capture
(blocked on Steven) · a shareable weekly card (good idea, wrong month — it is a
mini-league artefact and there are no mini-league conversations during the break).

**Confidence in this plan:** Medium–High. High that the sequencing is right given what
is known; medium overall, because every judgement in it rests on there being no
measurement at all — which is the first thing the plan fixes.
